What drives your number up or down

  • Heating. These figures assume gas heating. A home heated by electricity, such as storage heaters or a heat pump, can use two or three times as much.
  • People at home in the day. Working from home, a tumble dryer and an electric shower all push a 2-bed house towards the high figure.
  • Your region. Standing charges run from about 42p to over 66p a day depending on where you live, which alone moves the bill by up to £90 a year.
  • The car. An EV can add more electricity than the rest of a small flat uses. That is why the tariff you charge on matters more than almost anything else.

When an EV tariff makes the whole bill cheaper

EV tariffs give you a very cheap overnight rate, but charge more than the price cap during the day. If you have no car to charge, that usually makes the bill higher. Once a car is on the drive, the overnight savings on charging are far bigger than the extra the house pays by day. Run a dishwasher or washing machine on a timer in the cheap hours and the house saves too.

What January 2027 could do to your bill

Ofgem announces the price cap for 1 January to 31 March 2027 by 25 November 2026. Cornwall Insight's final forecast is 32.54p per kWh and about 56p a day. On those numbers a typical 2–3 bedroom house would pay about £1,018 a year for electricity instead of £858. Fixed EV tariffs keep their rates until the fix ends.

Questions

How much is the electricity bill for a 2 bedroom house in the UK?

Ofgem's typical figure for a 2–3 bedroom house is 2,500 kWh of electricity a year. On the price cap (26.32p per kWh and 54.83p a day standing charge, 1 October to 31 December 2026) that is about £858 a year, or £72 a month, for electricity alone. Gas is extra if you have it.

How much electricity does a 1 bedroom flat use?

Ofgem's low-use figure, for a flat or 1-bedroom home with one or two people, is 1,600 kWh a year: about £621 a year or £52 a month on the current price cap.

How much does an electric car add to my electricity bill?

Driving 7,000 miles a year in a car that does 3.5 miles per kWh needs about 2,222 kWh from the grid once charging losses are included. On the price cap that adds about £585 a year. On an overnight EV tariff at 6.66p it adds about £207, though the rest of the house then pays the tariff's higher day rate.

Is an EV tariff worth it if I only have a small house?

Usually yes if you charge at home. EV tariffs charge more than the price cap during the day, so the house costs a little more, but the car saves far more than that. Without an EV, or if you rarely charge at home, the price cap or a cheap fix is normally the better deal. Use the calculator above with your own numbers.

Will my electricity bill go up in January 2027?

If you are on a standard variable tariff, probably. Ofgem announces the January to March 2027 cap by 25 November 2026. Cornwall Insight's final forecast is 32.54p per kWh and about 56p a day, which would add roughly £160 a year to a typical 2–3 bedroom house's electricity bill. We will update this page once Ofgem publishes the new figures.
