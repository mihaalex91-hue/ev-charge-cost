British Gas Electric Driver tariff: what EV Power costs in 2026

British Gas's EV tariff used to be called Electric Driver. It's now sold as EV Power: 9.5p per kWh from midnight to 5am, every day, for the whole house. That makes overnight charging about a third of the price cap rate. It isn't the cheapest EV tariff, though, and the daytime rate and exit fee are worth checking before you switch.

What the British Gas EV tariff is

EV Power is a two-rate, electricity-only tariff for people who charge an electric car at home. For five hours a night, midnight to 5am, every unit you use costs 9.5p. That covers the car, the dishwasher, the washing machine and anything else on a timer. British Gas calls it 35 off-peak hours a week. The rest of the day you pay a higher peak rate.

If you've searched for "British Gas Electric Driver", this is the same product under its new name. Some comparison sites still use the old one, so check which tariff your quote is actually for. The current version is a fixed deal (MoneySavingExpert lists it as EV Power May28 v2), so your unit rates and standing charge stay the same for the fixed term.

New customers start on British Gas's standard variable tariff. Once the account is set up and the smart meter is sending readings, British Gas moves you to EV Power. Existing customers can switch in their online account.

How much does it cost to charge an electric car on it?

A typical overnight top-up looks like this: a car with a 60 kWh battery going from 10% to 80% needs 42 kWh in the battery. Allow about 10% for charging losses and that's roughly 47 kWh from the wall.

That charge adds about 147 miles to a car doing 3.5 miles per kWh, so about 3p a mile overnight against 8.4p on the price cap.

Over a year, 7,000 miles in the same car needs about 2,220 kWh from the wall. All charged overnight on EV Power, that's about £211 a year (£17.60 a month), against about £585 (£48.70 a month) on the price cap: a saving of around £374 on the car alone.

Will it fit in five hours? A 7 kW home charger delivers about 35 kWh in the window, roughly 31 kWh into the battery or about 110 miles. That covers most people's daily driving, but not a full 10→80% on a 60 kWh battery in one night. If you often come home nearly empty, either split the charge across two nights or look at a tariff with a longer window. A 3-pin cable at about 2.3 kW adds only about 11 kWh in five hours.

The catch: the daytime rate

Everything outside midnight–5am costs the peak rate, and on EV Power that's well above the price cap: about 38.5p on average against 26.32p. Take a household using 2,500 kWh a year for everything except the car, with 2,000 kWh of that during the day. It would pay about £243 more for that daytime electricity than on the cap. It would win back about £84 by moving 500 kWh of laundry and dishwashing into the cheap hours. So in this example the house costs about £160 more, and the net saving drops from about £374 to roughly £215 a year.

The more you drive and the less you use in the day, the better it looks. If you work from home with heavy daytime use and only do a few thousand miles a year, a standard fixed tariff could even work out cheaper. Who it suits

  • Existing British Gas customers who want an EV rate without changing supplier.
  • Hive charger owners. Power+ brings charging to an equivalent 7.125p, close to the cheapest tariffs.
  • Cars and chargers that smart tariffs don't support. EV Power has no compatibility list. Just set a timer for midnight to 5am.
  • Households that can shift laundry, dishwashers or a home battery into the cheap hours.

It suits you less if you use a lot of electricity during the day, regularly need more than about 30 kWh a night, or might switch within the fixed term and face the exit fee.

Tip: the window is midnight to 5am. Before you set your timer, check with British Gas whether those hours move when the clocks change for British Summer Time. Some suppliers' windows do, and charging an hour late would be billed at the peak rate.

Questions

What is the British Gas Electric Driver tariff?

Electric Driver was the old name for British Gas's EV tariff. Today it is sold as EV Power: a fixed, electricity-only, two-rate tariff with 9.5p per kWh between midnight and 5am every day, on everything the house uses in those hours.

What is the British Gas EV tariff off-peak rate?

9.5p per kWh from 12am to 5am, every day (British Gas, checked 17 November 2026). With a compatible Hive charger and Hive Power+, British Gas says EV charging works out at an equivalent 7.125p per kWh.

What is the British Gas EV Power peak rate and standing charge?

British Gas only shows them in a postcode quote. Independent trackers put the day rate at about 37–41p per kWh depending on region (MoneySavingExpert's average: 38.49p) and the standing charge at roughly 42–67p a day. Check your own quote.

Is there an exit fee on British Gas EV Power?

Yes. The current fixed version lists a £100 per fuel exit fee (MoneySavingExpert and Selectra, November 2026). It is not charged in the last 49 days of the fix or within the 14-day cooling-off period.

Do I need a smart meter or a particular charger?

You need a smart electricity meter (British Gas fits one free), to pay by Direct Debit and an EV you charge at home. Any make of car and any home charger works. Only the Hive Power+ discount needs a Hive-compatible charger.

How much does it cost to charge an electric car on British Gas EV Power?

Taking a 60 kWh battery from 10% to 80% needs about 47 kWh from the wall, which is about £4.43 at 9.5p, against about £12.28 at the 26.32p price cap. Driving 7,000 miles a year costs about £211 a year in charging.
