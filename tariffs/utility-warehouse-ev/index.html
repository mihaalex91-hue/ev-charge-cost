Key facts

  • Supplier: Utility Warehouse
  • Cheap window: 00:00–05:00 GMT (01:00–06:00 in summer time)
  • Eligibility: Smart meter and at least two other UW services (broadband, mobile or boiler cover)
  • Exit fee: None on energy; bundle services have their own terms
  • Smart meter: required — every time-of-use tariff needs one

The whole-house question. The cheap rate applies to everything you use in the window, not just the car — so a dishwasher or immersion heater on a timer also gets 6.9p. But everything outside the window is billed at 27.16p, above the price cap. A household using 3,000 kWh a year of daytime electricity would pay about £25 a year more on the house than on the cap; a moderate peak rate like this one only makes sense if the car saving is bigger than that.

Will your car fill up in the window? A 7 kW charger delivers about 6.3 kWh an hour into the battery. In 5 hours that is roughly 32 kWh, enough to add 110 miles at 3.5 mi/kWh — more than most people drive in a day, but not a full charge on a 75–80 kWh battery from empty. If you regularly arrive home nearly flat, a longer window or a smart tariff that adds slots matters more than a fraction of a penny per kWh.

Common questions

What is the Utility Warehouse EV off-peak rate?

6.9p per kWh, 00:00–05:00 gmt (01:00–06:00 in summer time). Outside the window electricity costs about 27.16p per kWh. Rates checked 29 September 2026.

Who can get Utility Warehouse EV?

Smart meter and at least two other UW services (broadband, mobile or boiler cover).

Does Utility Warehouse EV have an exit fee?

None on energy; bundle services have their own terms.

How much would I save with Utility Warehouse EV?

An 8,000-mile driver in a typical 3.5 mi/kWh EV, charging at home, pays about £18.89 a month for charging on Utility Warehouse EV against £56 on the price cap — around £442 a year. The rest of the house pays the peak rate outside the window, which can eat into the saving if you use a lot of daytime electricity.
